The inside of a neglected water heater tells the whole story. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, built to corrode in place of the tank, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. For homeowners in South Jordan, UT, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

The work is hands-on and specific. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

We maintain tankless units as well. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that restrict flow and reduce efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and look at the error history for patterns. Whatever type you have, you receive a plain summary of the unit's condition and any parts approaching the end of their life. For water heater maintenance in South Jordan, call (801) 998-0071.

How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

There is a short list of things you can do yourself: watching for leaks, keeping the area around the heater clear, and noting new noises. Beyond that, the work gets riskier. Draining a tank means handling scalding water and a drain valve that may not close again if it is old and full of sediment. Pulling an anode rod often takes a breaker bar and real leverage on a fitting that has not moved in years. Testing a T&P valve on an older heater can leave it dripping afterward. On gas units, the burner, thermocouple, and venting involve combustion safety, and a vent that is not drafting can put carbon monoxide into the house.

We come ready for the common surprises: a drain valve that will not reseal, an anode rod that has turned to wire, a relief valve that drips after testing. We carry replacements, along with meters for element and thermostat testing and a descaling kit for tankless units. More than parts, we bring a trained eye. Small signs, like rust at a connection, a weak flame, or a waterlogged expansion tank, point to future problems, and catching them early is the entire point of the visit. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Keep the thermostat near 120 degrees to limit scalding and slow sediment buildup
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
