Keeping Your Herriman Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one with water on the floor.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Herriman

Water heaters in and around Herriman face a familiar set of problems. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, minerals can be a real factor: calcium and magnesium drop out as sediment on the tank floor, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the popping and rumbling many homeowners hear during a heating cycle. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, and that's why regular descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A standard tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod has often been used up, leaving the tank exposed to corrosion. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: thermocouples, burners, and venting on gas units, and elements, thermostats, and breakers on electric ones.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well can see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, which a different anode rod often cures. A tankless unit on hard water needs annual descaling. And an older installation getting a new heater may need code items that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a correctly piped relief valve line, a drain pan, or strapping where required. All of these is fixable, and most are much cheaper when caught early. If something seems off, a quick diagnostic visit catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one.
