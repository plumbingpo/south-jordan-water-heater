How Sandy Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing discolored water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Sandy

In Sandy, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tanks usually give trouble through thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric tanks burn out heating elements and thermostats, especially when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters show error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they struggle in cramped closets. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
